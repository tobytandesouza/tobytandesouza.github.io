PEDAGOGY

Teaching difficult ideas without making them simplistic.

Good teaching does not reduce complexity. It makes complexity navigable, so that students can develop understanding rather than merely reproduce an answer.

01 · CLARITY

Make difficulty visible

I treat clarity as a form of intellectual precision. The aim is not to remove difficulty, but to show students where the difficulty lies and give them a way into it.

02 · CONTEXT

Teach the learner in front of you

Students do not arrive as abstract minds. Their histories, confidence, language, culture and relationship to education shape what good teaching requires.

03 · JUDGEMENT

Build intellectual independence

The strongest learning occurs when students can evaluate an explanation, recognise assumptions and make decisions about evidence without relying on authority alone.

01 · UNIVERSITY

Philosophy across disciplines

At the University of Queensland, I taught Introduction to Philosophy, Responsible Data Science, Ethics in Statistics and Philosophy of Science.

Teaching students from philosophy, science, engineering and data disciplines required translating abstract concepts without sacrificing their rigour. As Head Tutor, I also supported tutor coordination, moderation, assessment and detailed academic feedback.

Ideas are only as good as their ability to survive contact with students.

02 · SCHOOLS

Secondary English and curriculum

In Chiang Mai, I designed and taught differentiated English programs for multilingual secondary students, developed curriculum and assessment aligned with Common Core standards, and monitored progress through STAR360 and WIDA.

This work sharpened my attention to language, scaffolding and the difference between producing fluent work and genuinely understanding it.

Good scaffolding should create independence, not dependence.

03 · INDIVIDUAL LEARNING

Private tutoring

My current private tutoring work is individual, online and responsive to each student’s goals, assessment demands and learning needs.

I design personalised lessons in secondary English and humanities, with particular emphasis on reading comprehension, academic writing and critical reasoning.

One-to-one teaching makes misunderstanding visible quickly and demands precise adaptation.

04 · YOUTH PRACTICE

Learning in context

Youth work in Cooktown and at Bremer State High School changed how I understand education. Young people often encounter learning alongside instability, trauma, exclusion or disengagement.

That experience reinforced the importance of relationship, trust, context and institutional support. It also taught me that educational difficulty cannot always be separated from the conditions in which a student is trying to learn.

Students bring whole lives into classrooms.

Pedagogy after generative AI

Generative AI makes the distinction between fluent production and genuine understanding harder to ignore. My experience teaching Responsible Data Science, philosophy and secondary English has reinforced my view that education will need to place greater emphasis on judgement, authorship, explanation and intellectual independence.
