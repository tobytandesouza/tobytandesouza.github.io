The same object. Different relations.

A stone.

The object below will not change. Change only its context.

Among thousands of others, it is a stone on a beach.

The stone did not change. Its context did.
But is it still the same thing?

Context can do more than alter how something appears. Relations can help determine what something is: an ordinary stone, an artefact, a memorial, evidence. The question that has followed my work is whether context merely surrounds things, or partly constitutes their identity.

I came to that question through environmental philosophy and the philosophy of science, and it continues to shape my teaching. More recently, I have become interested in what it might contribute to questions about education and AI, especially when systems summarise, select and reorganise the contexts through which people encounter information.
